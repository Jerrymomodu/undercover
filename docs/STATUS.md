# Status

The claims pilot in `docs/PLAN.md`, the harness in `docs/PLAN_HARNESS.md`, durable state in `docs/PLAN_DURABLE.md` and the swarm in `docs/PLAN_SWARM.md` are built. Every criterion of the swarm's six phases is met but one: during a dispatch at N=25, a sign-off was paid after 13 seconds rather than within five (see Known gaps).

## Running it

- `npm run dev` starts the backend on port 8000, the agent's service on port 8002 and the dashboard on http://localhost:5173. It passes `.env` to the backend and the agent's service, so it needs a `.env`. With `DATABASE_URL` there the backend uses Supabase; the agent's service and `uv run -m agent` always need it, since they save the claim graph's progress there. Start a dispatch on the queue page and an evaluation on the Scoreboard page; both run in the agent's service, one at a time, on the real model through OpenRouter.
- `npm run seed` rebuilds the database `npm run dev` uses from `data/`, putting every claim back in the queue. `uv run -m backend.seed` alone never reads `.env` and rebuilds the SQLite file `undercover.db`, as the end-to-end tests do. The backend only seeds a database with no tables, so reseed after pulling a change to the tables: an older `undercover.db` lacks columns such as `runs.number`.
- `uv run -m agent CLM-1001` works one claim with the claim agent alone, outside any dispatch, and prints its report line. It uses the real model through OpenRouter and saves the claim graph's progress in Supabase. Without a claim id it prints its usage: dispatches are started through the agent's service.
- A payout over the limit waits for a handler to sign it off on its claim page. With `npm run dev` running, the agent's service then pays it within a second or two; otherwise the next dispatch does.

## Built

- `backend/`: the database is seeded from `data/` (never `data/evals/`) and created on startup when it has no tables. A test checks that every operation follows the API rules.
  - Database: SQLAlchemy Core, with the tables and every query written once in `backend/database.py` for both databases. With `DATABASE_URL` set, it is the Supabase Postgres database, through psycopg and the session pooler, with every table in the `undercover` schema (`search_path` is set on each connection, in place of any `options` in the URL and keeping its other settings, such as `sslmode`) and a pool of at most 4 connections. Otherwise it is SQLite at `undercover.db` or the path in `UNDERCOVER_DB`, with a connection per request. Each request runs in one transaction, committed before the response is sent. The tests and the end-to-end tests always use SQLite and never touch the network.
  - Read operations: `health`, `list_claims`, `get_claim`, `get_policy`, `get_claim_history`, `list_handlers`, `list_steps`, `get_instructions`, `search_documents`, `read_section`, `get_run`, `get_latest_dispatch`, `list_evaluations`, `get_evaluation`.
  - Payouts: `calculate_payout` works out the payout on a claim with UnderCover's payout rules in `backend/payout.py`, brought in from `labs/week2day1_payout.py`, and records nothing. Seeding stores `plans.json` in `settings`, and the approval limit comes from there too, so the rules read nothing from `data/`. Every input of the operation is required, including each field of a line.
  - Workflow operations: `start_claim`, `record_step`, `record_decision`, `pay_claim`, `sign_off_claim`, `fail_claim`, `record_model_call`, `record_work_time`. Runs: `start_dispatch` and `start_evaluation` (ask the agent's service through `backend/agent_service.py`, passing on its 202 or 409, or 503 when it can't be reached), `get_latest_dispatch`, `list_evaluations`, `get_evaluation`, `create_dispatch`, `create_evaluation`, `add_run_claims`, `get_run`, `finish_run`, `stop_runs`. A run carries its summary, its model cost and the agent's time, all from its own records.
  - Claims: a claim moves from waiting to in progress. A decline or a referral is then decided. An approval stays in progress until `pay_claim` pays it, which decides it, or moves to waiting for sign-off when it pays over the limit in `company.json`. `pay_claim` refuses such a payout with a 403 until a handler signs off. A sign-off records the handler and the time on the decision, moves the claim back to in progress and pays nothing. Once it is committed, a background task asks the agent's service to pick the claim up, through `backend/agent_service.py` at `UNDERCOVER_AGENT` or `http://127.0.0.1:8002`; if the service can't be reached, the backend logs a warning and the sign-off stands. The payment shows who signed off and when. A repeat of the decision or payment already recorded returns the claim, and a different one is refused with a 409, so a step that runs again after a restart does no harm. `pay_claim` returns the claim. Each start counts as an attempt. `fail_claim` records the reason as an `error` step and puts the claim back to waiting, or after its second attempt leaves it `left_for_handler`; it refuses a claim that has a decision.
  - Runs: a run is a dispatch or an evaluation, with N, the model and its reasoning effort, a status of running, finished or stopped, and its claims in the order it listed them (`run_claims`). Seeding drops the queue agent's old `todos` table. Each model call (`model_calls`) and each run of the claim graph (`work_times`) is recorded with its run, or none.
  - Documents: seeding stores the policy wording and the claims manual in a `documents` table with their titles, and the backend splits both into their numbered sections. `read_section` returns one section with its document's title, and `search_documents` finds the sections that mention a word or phrase.
  - Evaluations: seeding stores the held-out claims in `held_out_claims`, out of the queue, and the handlers' decisions on both labelled sets in `answer_key`; `database.py` is the only module that reads `data/evals/`. `create_evaluation` copies the labelled claims of the evaluation set (the queue's 25) or the held-out set, whole or just the five tiny ones, each with a new id such as `E3-CLM-1101` written into its record, `evaluation_id` and `copy_of`. Copies are left out of `list_claims`, a dispatch's claims can never include one, and `sign_off_claim` refuses one. `backend/scoring.py`, the only reader of the answer key, scores an evaluation from the records as in the team's labs, counting an undecided copy as a referral: decision accuracy, accuracy, the cost of mistakes, the straight-through rate, model cost and time per claim and the whole run's time.
  - Instructions: `backend/instructions/claim.md` for working a claim gives the agent its goal, what it has to work from and what done looks like, and leaves the steps to the agent and to the manual. `get_instructions` serves it, followed by the contents of the wording and the manual.
- `agent/`, in five parts: `api.py` is the agent's only way to the backend, with one function per operation, at `UNDERCOVER_API` or `http://127.0.0.1:8000`. `uv run -m agent` reads `UNDERCOVER_API` from the shell's environment, never from `.env`, because `api.py` reads it before the command line loads `.env`; the agent's service gets it from `.env` through `npm run dev`. `tools.py` holds every tool and the context and state they read. `harness.py` builds and runs the claim agent. `dispatcher.py` works a dispatch's claims. `service.py` is the agent's service. `uv run -m agent` loads `.env` and prints the claim's report line. The default model is `openai/gpt-6-luna` with low reasoning effort, a 120 second timeout and 5 retries, or the model in `MODEL_NAME`.
  - The claim agent is a hand-written LangGraph graph, built by `claim_graph(llm, saver, instructions)`, with a node that calls the model (`agent`), a `ToolNode` (`tools`), `wait_for_sign_off` and `pay`. Its state holds the messages, the model calls so far, and the decision and status the backend returned from `record_decision`. After a decision a decline or a referral ends the graph, an approval the backend can pay goes to `pay`, and one the backend holds for sign-off pauses at `wait_for_sign_off` with `interrupt()`; the graph never compares a payout with the limit itself. The agent node records every message and tool call as a step on the claim, and the tool node records every tool result plus a `reading` step for each section read. The backend's refusals and calls with bad arguments go back to the model, any other error stops the graph, and the agent node stops after 30 model calls, counted across restarts. Tools run one at a time, so two decisions in one reply can't race.
  - The claim agent has seven tools named after backend operations: `get_claim`, `get_policy`, `get_claim_history`, `search_documents`, `read_section`, `calculate_payout` and `record_decision`. They act on the claim in the agent's `ClaimContext`, so a claim agent can't touch another claim. It has no tool to pay: the graph pays.
  - Progress: the graph saves its state after every step (`durability="sync"`) with LangGraph's `PostgresSaver`. `connections()` opens the process's pool of at most 4 connections to `DATABASE_URL`, with the tables in the `undercover` schema beside the backend's, and claim agents wait for a connection rather than fail. `ClaimAgent.graph()` builds each claim run's graph on a saver of its own on that pool, since a saver holds a lock around every read and write. The agent is synchronous throughout, since async Postgres connections don't work with Windows' default event loop. The tests use an `InMemorySaver`.
  - Tracing: each claim agent run is traced in Langfuse with the keys in `.env`, in a session named after the claim and its thread, such as `CLM-1001:ca130bd0-...`, as a trace named "Work claim", or "Carry on" when it carries a claim on, tagged with its dispatch's or evaluation's id. The run is invoked in a fresh `contextvars.Context`, so it is a root run, never a subgraph of the dispatcher, which Langfuse needs to set the session. Without the keys, Langfuse prints a warning and sends nothing. The tests put a recorder in its place.
  - `work_claim(claim_id, graph, run_id)` starts the claim as a new attempt, with a new thread id, and runs the claim graph on that thread from a clean start. Any failure before the decision, or finishing without one, is recorded with `fail_claim`; a rate limit or a timeout goes through unsettled, for the dispatcher to wait out. A claim decided before a failure keeps its decision, the error is logged, and an approval whose payment failed stays in progress. It returns one line built from the backend's record, like `CLM-1001  approve  $420.00`.
  - `carry_on(claim_id, graph, run_id)` carries a claim in progress on from its saved progress, as the same attempt, on the same thread. A claim at the pause has been signed off, so it resumes past the pause and the graph pays it. A claim with nothing saved and no decision starts from the beginning on its thread. Any other carries on from its last saved step; the backend treats a repeated decision or payment as done. `work_on(claim_id, run, agent)`, which the dispatcher calls, chooses between the two.
  - The agent's service, `agent/service.py`, is a FastAPI app on port 8002 with `health`, `resume_claim`, `start_dispatch` and `start_evaluation`. `resume_claim` refuses with a 409 a claim that isn't in progress with a sign-off, and otherwise returns 202 and carries the claim on in a background task, printing the report line; a dispatch leaves that claim alone meanwhile. The service keeps the process's connections open while it runs and builds the claim agent, with its instructions, for each request. Only a dispatch looks for claims to carry on. `agent/console.py` sets up the report lines for both entry points.
  - The dispatcher, `agent/dispatcher.py`, is a LangGraph graph with no model and no checkpointer: `list_claims` lists every claim in progress and every waiting claim, oldest submitted first, leaving out claims the service is resuming after a sign-off, and records them on the run; `fan_out` sends each to `work_claim` with `Send`; `finish_run` finishes the run once every claim agent has finished. `max_concurrency` is N. `work_claim` calls `harness.work_on`, which carries a claim in progress on or starts a waiting one. A rate limit or a timeout that outlasts the model's retries goes through to the worker's `RetryPolicy`, which waits and carries the claim on as the same attempt; when its waits run out, its `error_handler` leaves the claim in progress for the next dispatch. Any other failure puts the claim back in the queue, and the worker tries it once more as a new attempt; a second failure leaves it for a handler. LangGraph is at 1.2.22, since in 1.2.12 an `error_handler` on concurrent `Send` tasks still let the error stop the graph.
  - For an evaluation, the dispatcher works the copies the backend made for it, and `work_on` deletes the saved progress of a copy held for sign-off, which counts as finished. The claim agent's `get_claim` leaves out a copy's `evaluation_id` and `copy_of`, so it works a copy as it would any claim.
  - The agent's service also has `start_evaluation`, and `start_dispatch`: it returns 202 and works the dispatch in a background task, refuses a second start with a 409 while a run is going, and marks a dispatch that fails as stopped. When it starts, it waits for the backend and marks any run still running as stopped.
- `dashboard/`: styled to `BRAND.md` in light and dark mode. The queue and a claim page refresh every two seconds.
  - The queue (`/`): counts by state, the dispatch panel and a line per claim. The dispatch panel starts a dispatch with 1 to 25 claim agents at once (3 to start), says why a start was refused, such as "The agent's service isn't running", and shows the latest dispatch's claims with where each stands, its model cost, the agent's time and, once it has finished, the whole run's time and a summary built from the records: approved, declined and referred, each payout waiting for sign-off, and each claim left for a handler, still in progress or not started.
  - A claim (`/claims/:claimId`): its policy, earlier claims, the decision and payment, the sections the agent read, the agent's steps as it works, including failed attempts, and a sign-off form for a held payout. After a sign-off it says "Signed off by Dana Whitfield at 2:05 PM. Waiting for the agent to pay it.", and the payment reads "Paid $1,350.00, signed off by Dana Whitfield at 2:05 PM". It says when a claim is back in the queue after a failed attempt or left for a handler.
  - The Scoreboard (`/scoreboard`): starts an evaluation of the evaluation set or the held-out set, whole or its five tiny claims, with 1 to 25 claim agents at once, and lists every evaluation, newest first, with its set, N, model, effort, state and measures. An evaluation's page (`/scoreboard/:runId`) shows the measures and each copy with the handler's decision beside the agent's, the misses marked in Raspberry, and a link to the copy's page, which has no sign-off form.
  - The claim agent's instructions (`/instructions`), read only.
- `e2e/`: tests of the three pages, the agent's steps appearing on a claim page, a handler's sign-off reaching a stand-in for the agent's service (`e2e/agent_stand_in.ts`, on port 8003), a claim that fails twice being left for a handler, a dispatch and an evaluation started on the dashboard reaching the stand-in with N, the dispatch panel following a dispatch played through the API, and the scoreboard and an evaluation's page showing an evaluation played through the API. Playwright runs its own backend on port 8001 with `e2e/undercover.db`, and its own dashboard on port 5174, so it can run next to `npm run dev`.

## Known gaps

- A sign-off during a dispatch at N=25 was paid 13 seconds later, not within five. At rest it pays within five seconds. The backend's 4 connections are the queue: one round trip from this machine to Supabase takes about 180 ms, so each request holds a connection for close to a second, and 18 claim agents keep more requests coming than 4 connections can serve. A simple `get_claim` took 2 to 7 seconds under that load, against 0.3 seconds at rest. Since then each claim agent fetches its instructions once for a whole dispatch, rather than once per claim, which takes one heavy request per claim off the backend; that hasn't been tried live yet. Dropping `pool_pre_ping` and recording steps in fewer requests would cut the load further.
- For the same reason, claims worked from the start took about 245 seconds each at N=25, against about 67 seconds at N=3. The run as a whole was still much faster.
- The scoreboard's list takes about 2 seconds against Supabase, since each evaluation costs several queries.
- A claim with a decision but nothing saved on its thread, which only happens when someone approves a claim through the API by hand, can't be carried on: each run logs the error and leaves it in progress.
- If two processes carry the same claim on at the same moment, which the harness can't rule out, the backend still decides and pays it once. The second process's decision or payment, arriving while the first is being saved, is refused with an error, and that process logs it.
- A handler can only sign off a held payout; rejecting one comes later.

## Supabase

The backend's tables and the checkpointer's are in the `undercover` schema. Row Level Security is off on them, and Supabase's table listing warns about that, but the schema isn't exposed through Supabase's Data API: the `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role` roles have no access to it, and Supabase's security advisor reports nothing. The backend and the agent connect as `postgres`, the tables' owner.

## Review of the harness

On September 30, 2026, the head of engineering's review (`docs/HUMAN_REVIEW_HARNESS.md`) found that the harness was unfit for a PR:
- The agent had more than 20 hard-coded backend URLs and two ways of calling the backend.
- `queue.py` reached into `main.py`.
- The tools were spread across files, and state was passed through closures instead of LangChain's `context_schema`.
- The instructions were step-by-step scripts.

The agent was rebuilt as `api.py`, `tools.py` and `harness.py`, and the instructions were rewritten as goals. Fresh reviewers then went through the backend, the dashboard and the tests, and their fixes are in the same change.

These problems passed every check. Each phase was built, checked against its criteria and committed, and nobody read the code the way a reviewer reads a PR. `docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md` now sets the structural rules the review found broken. `docs/PROCESS.md` now requires a review by a fresh subagent before every commit, and a design in every plan.

With CLM-1003, CLM-1004 and CLM-1012 in progress, every process listening on port 8002 was killed (uvicorn's reloader and its worker, with `taskkill /F /T`), and `npm run dev` stopped the backend and the dashboard with it. Its summary showed 4 approved, 2 declined, CLM-1008 ($2,240.00) and CLM-1011 ($1,220.00) waiting for sign-off, the three claims still in progress, and 16 not started.

It finished in 4 minutes 52 seconds, against 4,175 seconds of claim time added up, for $0.0177 in model calls. No claim failed or was left for a handler, and none failed for want of a database connection. Supabase showed at most 11 connections as `postgres`, counting the query that checked. Through Langfuse's observations API, filtering by the dispatch's id as a tag found exactly its 19 claims.
- CLM-1008, signed off as Dana Whitfield during that dispatch, was paid 13 seconds later (see Known gaps). CLM-1011, signed off as Priya Raman with nothing else running, was paid within five seconds. CLM-1003 is still waiting for sign-off.

An evaluation of the held-out set's five tiny claims at N=5 finished in 93 seconds: decision accuracy 100%, accuracy 100%, no mistakes, 80% straight through. Both appear on the scoreboard. The queue showed 24 decided and 1 waiting for sign-off before and after them, and the three copies held for sign-off have no saved progress left.
